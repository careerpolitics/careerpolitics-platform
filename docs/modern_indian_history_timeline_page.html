1600–1707: Entry and Expansion

  • Company trading factories grow in Surat, Madras, Bombay, and Calcutta.
  • Mughal decline opens opportunities for regional and European competition.

1757–1818: Company State Formation

  • Plassey (1757), Buxar (1764), and Diwani rights (1765) secure revenue base.
  • Maratha and Mysore wars reshape political control across India.

1818–1856: Reform and Resistance

  • Administrative codification, Western education, and social reform debates.
  • Doctrine of Lapse and annexations increase discontent.

1857–1885: Crown Raj Begins

  • Revolt of 1857 transforms governance and army recruitment policy.
  • Railways, telegraph, and bureaucracy expand centralized rule.

1885–1919: Early Nationalism

  • Congress moderates seek constitutional reform; extremists demand swaraj.
  • Swadeshi movement links economic self-reliance and political protest.

1919–1935: Gandhian Mass Politics

  • Non-Cooperation, Salt March, and Civil Disobedience involve rural/urban masses.
  • Government of India Act 1935 introduces provincial autonomy framework.

1935–1947: War and Decolonization

  • World War II pressures empire; Quit India (1942) intensifies resistance.
  • Cabinet Mission, negotiations, and Partition lead to Independence (1947).

1947–1950: Nation Building

  • Integration of princely states and refugee rehabilitation.
  • Constitution adopted (1949), effective 26 January 1950.
